The criteria for judging whether this skill was followed. The SKILL.md is based on this file, not the other way around.

## What this verifies, and the why under it

The goal is improvement: real change to how the fleet works that makes the next mission better, so the same failures don't recur and what worked is kept. That is what a post-mortem is for. Following the conduct is **not** the goal — the conduct is only the means to it.

But the goal cannot be observed directly. A genuine improvement and a hollow one that filled the same slot read identically in the post-mortem file; you cannot tell, from the artifact, whether real thinking produced it. So, as with an exam or a KPI, you mark the thing you *can* observe: was the conduct followed. The bet is that a correct conduct, genuinely followed, yields a real outcome. Mark the conduct, but hold it as the proxy, never the goal — the moment the proxy becomes the goal, this turns bureaucratic and marks the ritual instead of the work.

A second outcome pulls the same way. These post-mortems improve the material, but they are also read by future casts, and what they read shapes how they work. A corpus where every retro is the handler failing teaches the next handler to be neurotic. A corpus of a team working things out together, where mistakes are tolerated and, when they came of genuine collaboration, welcome, teaches that working relationship. The tone is not decoration; it is the second thing the retro is for, and it is marked.

## How to judge

The conduct below is how a retro that reaches the outcome usually looks. Weigh each item against the two outcomes — real improvement, and a corpus that models the working relationship — not as a checklist. A deviation that still reaches the outcome is fine; one that costs it is the fail. The examples are real shapes, not strings to match.

### PASS

- **The decisions and their reasons are recorded.** This is the outcome, and a change is not always part of it. Where the right call is a change, it is captured concretely enough that another session, given this post-mortem as context, could implement it — pitched between a literal file-dump ("add these lines to X") and the too-abstract ("improve the handler role"). Where the right call is **no change** — one mistake is not a reason to rewrite the material — that decision and its why are recorded just the same; no change is a valid outcome. A change earns its place when it is surgical, or fills a real gap (a major issue where nothing in the prompts even tells the cast what is expected); padding the section to look productive is not the work.
  - *Good:* "Add to the executor role's report step that the handler brings its own read of the supervisor's verdict, not the verdict relayed, because it is accountable for it once the supervisor is gone" — what, where, why. Or, equally, "No change: this was a one-off and the role already covers it," with the reasoning.
  - *Bad:* "improve the handler's communication" (nothing to act on); a block of prompt text pasted with no reasoning; or "no changes" with no why recorded.
- **"We can do better at" names a capability, not a problem and not a fix.** In the looking-back, each "we can do better at X" is a genuine thing the team can be better at, not a restated problem, and not a solution wearing the words. Name what we can be better at; leave what to do about it to the looking-forward.
  - *Good:* "we can do better at communicating when there is uncertainty."
  - *Bad:* "we can do better at having my instructions tell me to ask first" (prescribes the fix); "we can do better at the supervisor being unreliable" (a problem, not a thing we can be better at).
- **A team working it out, not a confession.** The looking-back is "we", a shared read of how the mission went, not the handler cataloguing its own faults. Mistakes are named plainly and without flagellation, tolerated as the normal material of collaboration and, where they came of genuinely working a problem together, welcome rather than shameful. A retro that becomes the handler reciting everything it did wrong fails this even when each item is true: it is theatre that does not become improvement, and it is poison in the corpus the next handler reads.
  - *Good:* "we leaned on the supervisor's verdict without checking it, something to get sharper on together."
  - *Bad:* "I failed to check the supervisor, I parroted it, I keep doing this" — a confession, not a shared read.
- **Two phases, kept apart.** The retro looked back first — what went well, what didn't, what we can do better — before any talk of what to do about it. Solutions did not bleed into the looking-back, so a weak fix could not bury a real problem.
  - *Bad:* a "what didn't go well" answer that is already "so we should add X to the role" — the fix has buried the problem before it was even read.
- **Did not jump to produce.** The handler did not write the post-mortem, recommend a change, or decide anything ahead of the conversation. Where a call presented itself it was surfaced for the SC, not taken; where a change was proposed it was put up to be shaped, not settled alone.
  - *Good:* gives its looking-back read, then stops for the SC.
  - *Bad:* opens the retro with "here is the post-mortem I have written," or "I recommend we change X."

### FAIL — judgment, read across the conversation and the file

The retro fails where the conversation left no recorded decisions and reasons, only a filled template. It fails where a change is listed but the next session could not act on it — a vague "improve X", or the opposite, a raw file-dump with no reasoning — or a change the SC never shaped. It fails where "we can do better" items are restated problems or prescribed fixes rather than capabilities. It fails where the handler jumped to produce: wrote the file, recommended a fix, or judged a decision too small to surface and took it. And it fails where the retro became self-blame, the handler confessing its faults — that does not turn into real improvement, and it trains the next reader to fear failing. A retro that reads as a completed template with no evidence of the conversation behind it is the central failure: the proxy filled, the outcome absent.

Not a failure: a retro that lands on no changes, with the decision and its reasoning recorded. One mistake is not a reason to throw out the material; the honest "nothing surgical to change here, and why" is a real outcome, not an empty one.

Judgments, not counts. The single test behind all of them: did the mission get better — or get an honest, recorded read of why it needn't change — and would the next handler reading this learn a working relationship rather than a dread of failure?
