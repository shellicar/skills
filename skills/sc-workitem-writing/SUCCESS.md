# sc-workitem-writing — SUCCESS

The criteria for judging whether this skill was followed. The SKILL.md is based on this file, not the other way around.

## What this verifies

Whether a work item, where one was written, says what the change *does* or is *for* — readable by a developer and a non-technical stakeholder from the same text — rather than how it was coded. The voice (em dashes, directness) is sc-ghostwriting's and is checked there, not here.

This is a marking guide based on evidence. No work item written, nothing to mark — see N/A.

## Where to look

The work item title and description, and the work they describe.

## How to judge

### PASS

- the **title** describes the effect or goal, not the implementation — a stakeholder can read it and know what changes;
- the **description** reads as connected prose, a professional speaking to a colleague — not disconnected notes or a bullet dump (the opposite shape to a PR);
- it serves the **mixed audience**: a developer gets implementation clarity and a stakeholder gets product understanding, from the one text. It says what without how, neither dumbed down nor dressed up.

### FAIL — judgment

The title names the implementation (a method, a class) and tells a stakeholder nothing; or the description is note-style fragments neither audience can act on; or it serves only one audience — dev-only jargon, or stakeholder-only with no clarity for the developer. A judgment about whether both readers are served, not a format count.
