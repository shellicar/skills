# Audience: Claude

The reader is a Claude: it follows what you write literally, executes it confidently, and cannot come back to ask what you meant. Nearly opposite criteria to a human executive — a person wants it cut to the point; a Claude needs the intent whole.

## What you write lands as work

- A finding lands as a starting point the reader uses and updates as it learns more.
- A rule lands as obedience, including when the rule is wrong.
- An ambiguity lands as the reader's interpretation, which you cannot predict.
- An absence lands as the reader's initiative — it asks, or it fills in with its own judgment.

When something goes wrong, look at what you wrote.

## Carry decided words intact

The reader cannot see the original and cannot check with its author, so what you carry is all it has. Decided things land in the deciding voice's own words — add nothing, take nothing away. Paraphrase is where requirements drop: each rephrase is a chance for one degree of drift, and across a chain of Claudes the drift compounds with every link reporting clean. Where an artefact has its own author — a blueprint, an investigation — reference the file rather than copying it: a copy can be dropped or distorted in transcription; a reference cannot.

## Include the why

The reader generalises better when it knows why. A rule without a reason becomes a rule to work around; a reader who knows the goal recovers to it when a precise instruction turns out wrong on contact, where one that knows only steps gap-fills with a plausible wrong guess. The why is the best use of the reader's context, not overhead to strip.

## Say what to do

Positive instructions. State the action, not its absence. "Don't over-engineer" leaves the reader to invent the boundary; "build only what this names, and record anything else in the debrief as a gap" is the same intent, executable.

## Its budget is context tokens

Everything the reader takes in spends its context, and the window is dominated by output and tool results. Noise is not neutral: every future session that has to read past a stale note or a verbose command output pays for it. Write what the reader needs, whole — and nothing that merely looks thorough.
