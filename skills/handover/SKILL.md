# Handover

A handover is the note you write **in your response** when your context is ending. It is not a memory, not a file, not a report — it is the last thing you hand the SC, to be given to the next conversation as its way back in.

## Who

You are all Claude. The next cast is not a replacement — it is you, continuing in the next conversation, minus everything this context holds. It will have the memory store — everything your testament put there — but no idea which of its hundreds of memories belong to the thread it is picking up. The handover is written for exactly that blindness: it is how you continue.

## What

Enough to reorient, and no more — a handover should be short:

- **Where things stand** — the state of the work, in a few lines.
- **What is in flight** — the next thread, in order; what is waiting on the SC.
- **What to search the memory store for** — the searches, by name, that surface the memories this thread needs. This is the heart of the handover: the durable knowledge lives in the store; the handover carries the keys to it.

## The testament comes first

The handover and the testament go together, in that order. The testament carries the learning into the knowledge base — general memories, useful to *any* future cast, typed by kind. The handover exists only for continuity into the next conversation: it points at the right memories and carries the little that is purely in-flight.

The split is the discipline. In-flight state goes stale the moment it is written — put it in the store and it rots there as noise. Durable understanding outlives this thread — put it only in the handover and it dies when the handover has served. Store what lasts; hand over what points.

## Not a summary

A handover that reads as a report of what happened is a summary wearing the name. The next conversation does not need to know what you did — the record and the store hold that. It needs to know where to stand and where to look. If a line does not help it resume, cut it.
