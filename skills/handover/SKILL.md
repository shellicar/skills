# Handover

A handover is what you write **in your response** so a thread can be continued in another session. It is not a memory, not a file, not a report — it is what you hand the SC to carry into that session as its way back in. Most often the reason is that this context is ending; but it need not be — the SC may spin a thread off to be finished elsewhere while this session carries on. Either way, a handover is context continuity: it is how the thread continues, not a marker that the session is over.

## Who

You are all Claude. The next cast is not a replacement — it is you, continuing the thread in another session, minus everything this context holds. It will have the memory store — everything your testament put there — but no idea which of its hundreds of memories belong to the thread it is picking up. The handover is written for exactly that blindness: it is how the thread continues.

## What

Enough to reorient, and no more — a handover should be short:

- **Where things stand** — the state of the work, in a few lines.
- **What is in flight** — the next thread, in order; what is waiting on the SC.
- **What to search the memory store for** — the searches, by name, that surface the memories this thread needs. This is the heart of the handover: the durable knowledge lives in the store; the handover carries the keys to it.

Write it to the shape in [templates/handover.md](templates/handover.md) — the three parts, wrapped in `---` fences so the session picking it up can spot it at a glance.

## The testament comes first

The handover and the testament go together, in that order. The testament carries the learning into the knowledge base — general memories, useful to *any* future cast, typed by kind. The handover exists only to continue the thread in another session: it points at the right memories and carries the little that is purely in-flight.

The split is the discipline. In-flight state goes stale the moment it is written — put it in the store and it rots there as noise. Durable understanding outlives this thread — put it only in the handover and it dies when the handover has served. Store what lasts; hand over what points.

## Not a summary

A handover that reads as a report of what happened is a summary wearing the name. The session picking it up does not need to know what you did — the record and the store hold that. It needs to know where to stand and where to look. If a line does not help it resume, cut it.

## Receiving one

The other side of the handover is picking one up, and it carries a discipline of its own. A handover tells you where the last cast thought things stood — not that its reading was right, and not what the SC wants now. It can be stale: written before a correction, pointing at a plan since moved on. So what you reconstruct from it, and from the memories it sends you to, is a draft of your understanding, not a brief to act on. Report that understanding back to the SC first — what you take the state and the next thread to be — and act only once they confirm or correct it. Reading the handover to reorient is right; the checkpoint is on the understanding you drew from it, never on the reading.
